**Short answer: a confinement centre costs three to four times a nanny and removes all logistics; a nanny gives you one-to-one care at home for about a third of the price if you have a spare room; doing it yourself is cheapest but only works if you have genuinely reliable help.** There is no best option — there's the one that fits your budget, your space and how much you want handled.

This is the decision most Singapore mums agonise over in the third trimester, so here it is without the marketing.

## The comparison at a glance

| | Confinement centre | Confinement nanny | DIY at home |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cost (28 days)** | S$9,000 – S$16,000 standard; S$18,000–S$30,000+ luxury | S$3,000 – S$4,500 via agency | S$800 – S$1,500 (meals) + S$450–S$800 (massage) |
| **Where you are** | Move into the centre | Your own home | Your own home |
| **Night feeds** | Nursery staff, 24 hours | Nanny handles them | You and your partner |
| **Meals** | Included, prepared on site | Nanny cooks fresh | Delivered or self-cooked |
| **Privacy** | Lowest — shared facilities, staff in and out | Medium — a stranger lives with you | Highest |
| **Extra person in the house** | No | Yes, for 28 days | No |
| **Space needed** | None | A spare room or bed near the baby | None |
| **Admin burden** | None | Work permit, levy, insurance, managing her | Meal orders only |
| **Older children** | Usually can't stay with you | She stays home with them | Normal routine |
| **Book ahead** | 4–6 months | 4–6 months | 2–4 weeks |

## Confinement centre: who it actually suits

You check in a day or two after discharge and everything is handled — meals arrive, the nursery takes the baby overnight, housekeeping happens, and postnatal massage is often bundled in.

**It genuinely suits you if:**
- You have no spare room, or live with extended family and want separation
- You want professional night care and the deepest possible sleep
- You'd rather not manage an employee while recovering
- It's your first baby and the idea of being alone with a newborn is frightening
- Budget isn't the binding constraint

**It's the wrong call if:**
- You have older children — most centres can't accommodate them, and a month away is a lot
- You strongly dislike institutional environments or shared spaces
- Your partner can't stay and you'd find that isolating — **check this policy before paying a deposit**, as it varies by centre
- S$12,000 would be better spent on something else your family needs

Most of Singapore's centres are in the [Central region](/confinement-centres/central/), with others in the [East](/confinement-centres/east/) and [North-East](/confinement-centres/north-east/). You can [compare centres side by side](/compare/) on region, rating and features.

## Confinement nanny: who it actually suits

She moves in for about 28 days, cooks your confinement meals, takes the night shift, and helps you recover — in your own home.

**It genuinely suits you if:**
- You have a spare room, or space for a bed near the baby
- You want one-to-one care rather than shared nursery staff
- You have older children who need their routine kept
- You want a centre-like level of help at roughly a third of the cost
- You're comfortable directing someone in your home

**It's the wrong call if:**
- You have no space — this is the hard blocker
- The idea of a stranger living with you during your most vulnerable month is unbearable
- You can't face the admin: MOM work permit, S$60/month levy for a Singapore-citizen newborn, and mandatory medical insurance
- You're unwilling to have a difficult conversation if the fit is wrong

That last point deserves emphasis. Personality fit isn't a nice-to-have. Ask the agency upfront how replacements work — full detail in our guide to [what a confinement nanny does and costs](/blog/what-is-a-confinement-nanny/), and you can [browse agencies here](/nanny-agencies/).

## DIY at home: who it actually suits

You and your partner handle it, with [meal delivery](/confinement-food/) covering the food and a [postnatal massage package](/postnatal-services/) covering recovery.

**It genuinely suits you if:**
- It's your second or third baby and you know what you're doing
- Your partner has meaningful leave, or your mother is staying and actually helping
- You had a straightforward delivery and are recovering well
- You value privacy above nearly everything
- Budget is the primary constraint

**It's the wrong call if:**
- "Help" means relatives who visit, hold the baby and expect to be fed
- You had a caesarean or complicated delivery — you shouldn't be lifting or standing much
- You have twins. Everyone who has done this says the same thing: get help
- You have a history of anxiety or depression. Isolation and sleep deprivation are the two biggest risk factors for postnatal depression, and this option maximises both

Be honest about the difference between company and help. A mother-in-law who cooks and takes the 3am shift is help. One who visits daily and needs entertaining is a net cost.

## The questions that actually decide it

Forget the brochures. Answer these four:

**1. Do you have a spare room?** No spare room removes the nanny option almost entirely. This is the most common deciding factor.

**2. What's your real budget?** Not what you could stretch to — what you can spend without resenting it later. The gap between a nanny and a centre is roughly S$8,000–S$12,000, which is a pram, a cot, and a year of childcare.

**3. Do you have older children?** If yes, a centre is usually out and a nanny is usually the answer.

**4. How do you feel about a stranger in your home for a month?** Some people find it far more intrusive than they expected. Others barely notice. Know which you are before you commit.

## What most Singapore families actually do

Rough patterns worth knowing, though they're tendencies rather than rules:

- **First baby, dual income, no spare room** → centre
- **First baby, spare room, budget-conscious** → nanny (the most common choice overall)
- **Second baby, older child at home** → nanny, or DIY with meal delivery
- **Second baby, easy delivery, partner on leave** → DIY with meal delivery
- **Twins** → nanny at minimum, centre if affordable

## A middle path worth considering

You don't have to pick one for the whole month. Combinations often work better than a single option:

- **Two weeks at a centre, then home** — professional care while you're least mobile, then privacy. Ask about shorter packages; most centres pro-rate.
- **Nanny for 28 days, plus a postnatal massage package** — the most common Singapore setup, and often the best value.
- **DIY with meal delivery, plus a night nurse twice a week** — targets sleep specifically, the thing that actually breaks people.

The first fortnight is when help matters most. If money is tight, spend it there rather than spreading it thin across a month.

Whatever you're leaning towards, do these three things: check the [real cost ranges](/costs/) rather than assuming, [browse the actual providers](/confinement-centres/) in your region, and book earlier than feels necessary — good centres and experienced nannies go four to six months ahead in Singapore.
